StackingWizard Beta 2.3: the list that cannot die

  • The frame list is rebuilt from the ground up. The old list drew one widget per frame and simply gave up past a few hundred - rows went invisible, suspects vanished off the bottom, and four of you reported it four different ways. The new list draws only what is on screen, so two thousand frames scroll like twenty. Long filenames trim in the middle (the ends are what tell frames apart) and hovering shows the full name.
  • A UI Scale dial, top left, 90 to 140 percent, remembered between launches - for 1440p screens, 4K screens, and eyes of every age. The previews now render at your screen’s true resolution at any scale, and the layout holds still while a stack builds.
  • Headers that arrive broken get repaired on reading. Some cameras write illegal header cards - one tester’s QHY put its colour pattern down without the quote marks the FITS standard requires, and every build until now refused the whole night. His own frame proved the cure and now lives in the wizard’s test bench. And when a frame truly cannot be read, it is marked unreadable in red and set aside - never again graded 70 by a table of made-up numbers.
  • The Dwarf family is spoken here. Dwarf 3 and Dwarf Mini never label their frames, so their nights landed as “unidentified”. The wizard now recognises them by their own naming, reads their nested dark libraries, survives their two cameras sharing one calibration folder, and takes exposure times from filenames that state them.
  • The manifest says what it found, before you stack it - mixed targets, mixed filters, mosaic panels, duplicate copies, and where an exposure time came from. One test folder held two targets in three filters plus a finished stack and a rejected frame; every one now lands where it belongs, and the mix is declared up front.
  • The finished stack looks like the night you watched. The live view and the final result now share one display ruler from first frame to last - same brightness, same colours, same smoothness, at every zoom and every scale. What you watched build is what you get.
  • The hot-pixel warning wakes earlier - a night where only some frames lock onto sensor defects (stacked unmoved, showing as doubled stars) now gets named at three frames, where the old warning waited for most of the night to be lost.

Thank you Kevin C., Des F., Charlie M., Russ McN., Denis H., Morgan H. and Richard D. - and the test folders full of deliberate traps that found two more bugs before any of you could. Keep them coming: wizard@lukomatico.com

26 AUGUST 2026

StackingWizard Beta 2.2: the honesty build

First, a correction. Beta 2.1’s notes said hot pixels could no longer pose as stars. That was true for mono cameras and wrong for colour ones, where processing spreads a hot pixel into exactly the shape our filter trusts - one tester proved it within a day. The wizard now at least confesses when an alignment is impossibly perfect instead of celebrating it, and the real cure is being built for Beta 3. We will keep correcting ourselves out loud like this - it is a beta, and things breaking is rather the point.

  • Every frame stacks exactly once - one tester’s folder had been through another program first, so a second copy of every sub sat in a sub-folder, and the wizard stacked the lot: 48 “frames” that were 24 exposures twice each, a result overstated by 41 percent, and double the time and disk to pay for it. Two frames from one camera can never share a timestamp, so now they cannot share a seat - and the manifest says how many copies were set aside.
  • The auto-crop is gone - it existed to hide scruffy edges, and we patched it twice, but a meridian flip taught us there are nights no percentage can referee: it cropped a 5496-pixel sensor to a 434-pixel strip and handed that to AstroWizard without a word. Rather than destroy pictures quietly, the wizard now ships your full canvas, honest edges and all, and names a meridian flip when it sees one. Cropping is an editing decision, and it belongs in AstroWizard, where you can see what you are choosing.
  • The Pile stops guessing - what the header says now outranks what the filename says, which outranks what the folder is called, so a file named dark can never be swallowed by a folder named lights. The old “longer than five seconds is probably a light” rule is deleted: frames with no identification are counted as unidentified and said so. And the four count tiles are now buttons - point any pile at its own folder and your word is law. Thank you Frank L. for the idea.
  • The safety copy asks before outliving its purpose - every finished stack autosaves so nothing is lost to a crash or a forgotten click. But once your real save succeeds, that copy has done its job - four runs left one tester 380 MB of identical twins sitting among his subs. The wizard now asks, once: Remove or Keep, and remembers your answer.
  • The finished view stops dimming at the finish line - the completed stack now renders with the same sky brightness as the live view you watched it build at, however deep the night, and zooming in no longer changes the stretch. Extra depth shows as smoothness, never darkness.
  • Smaller honesties - the receipt’s “2.5% rejected” was pixels clipped by the voting, not frames thrown away, and now says so. Lights on a network drive get named as the slow part before you wait an hour to find out. The Handoff grew its own copy-the-receipts button. Shift-click ticks a whole run of frames at once, and the mood switch warns before re-grading over hand-made picks.
  • For Mac users - handing off to an AstroWizard that is already open should no longer stall. We cannot test this on hardware we do not own, so this one is an attempt, not a promise - if yours still loses its way, tell us.

Thank you to Mike N., whose saved log held two bugs he had not even reported, and to Frank L., Volker H., RickJ, Mike A., Sigurd M. and Dawid G. - this build is made of your emails. Keep them coming: wizard@lukomatico.com

25 AUGUST 2026

StackingWizard Beta 2.1: what the testers found

  • Hot pixels can no longer pose as stars - a sensor’s dead pixels sit in the same place on every frame, so on soft or defocused data the wizard could match them perfectly, compute an alignment that moved nothing, and stack the whole night unshifted while cheerfully reporting a fine result. A real star lights its neighbours; a dead pixel does not, and now only the former counts. An impossibly perfect alignment is reported as a warning rather than a triumph.
  • Blank frames stop winning - our idea of “best” was really “least noisy”, so a frame with nothing in it beat every real one, became the reference, and left nothing able to match it. One tester’s observatory dome was shut for some frames and his night collapsed to five of forty-four. A finished stack that wandered into a lights folder did the same thing. Neither can lead now.
  • Network drives work again - the working files created in Beta 2 arrived on a NAS with their permissions locked down and shut the wizard out of its own cache. They now live on a local disk, made the ordinary way. The space needed is shown on The Stack page before you begin, and the quiet drop to half precision when disk was tight is gone entirely - if there is no room, the wizard says how much it needs and where.
  • Your stacks know where they were pointing - saved files now carry coordinates, date, focal length, pixel size, camera, filter and a proper frame count and integration time. They plate solve, and other programs can tell a colour stack is colour. And a colour camera whose filter pattern we cannot read is stacked as mono out loud instead of silently.
  • One wandering frame no longer crops your picture in half - the crop asked that every single frame cover a pixel; it now asks nine in ten, for single nights and for combining.
  • The column headings stay put while the list scrolls, which seven of you asked for - and it turns out those columns have been sortable all along, which nobody could discover while the headings slid away.
  • The wizard explains itself - when a brightening sky sets frames aside it says so, and when one frame was exposed quite differently from the rest of the night it names it. Previews stop shrinking as the stack builds, the running tally is back beside the time remaining, and there is no more name.fits.fits.
  • A known-issues list now sits on the StackingWizard page, so you can see what is confirmed and still being worked on. This is a beta and things breaking is rather the point - thank you to Aaron, Phil, Helen, Sigurd, Marcos, Charlie, Karl, Mitja, Frédéric, Mike, Craig, Alex, Daz, Joaquin, Volker, Mario, Elliott, Mitja, Bill, Tom and everyone else who took the trouble to write it down.

23 AUGUST 2026

StackingWizard Beta 2: big nights

  • Big nights, fixed at the root - the first beta kept every aligned frame in memory, so a 500-frame Seestar night could eat 40 GB and crawl into swap on Windows and Mac alike. Aligned frames now stream through a disk cache that is freed afterwards, memory stays flat whatever the frame count, and the final vote is a new sort-once algorithm running on every core. measured on the workshop PC: 187 Seestar frames in 2 minutes 31 seconds end to end, where the first beta needed over 8 minutes for the final vote on 60 frames alone. Thank you Dean, Marco, Paul, Volker, Alex, and the Dwarf 3 M31 reporter - your receipts drew the map.
  • Never silent again - grading, aligning and the final vote each show a measured time left. The "half a minute or so" line was a lie at 521 frames and is gone. A Stop button stands while stacking.
  • Stricter alignment - six stars must agree before a frame is placed (four-star fits stacked extra galaxy cores on a Dwarf 3 M31 night); a frame gets a second, harder look before it is given up; frames a Dwarf itself marked "failed_" never lead the stack.
  • Small things you asked for - the copy-the-receipts button sits in the card where no window width can hide it; previews fit the panel (portrait Seestar frames showed as a zoomed crop); saved names carry the frame count; "stacked", not "master", in the header; combining leaves out a stack that shares under half the field instead of shrinking your result to a strip; grading folds frames that arrive at a different bit-scale (the S30 Pro "300% sky" frames) before any verdict.
  • Known, and next - the Looking Glass list struggles past about 500 frames (grading itself is fine; a lighter list is next), then the uncropped/union canvas and a calibration library. Defender still frowns at unsigned betas: the false-positive report for this build is filed and signing is in progress.

5 AUGUST 2026

The Steady Eyepiece

  • Your screen no longer “goes crazy” after noise reduction. The preview aims its screen-stretch using the image’s own statistics - so when NXT or GraXpert wiped away the noise, the view would suddenly dig far deeper and light up like a different image. Nothing was ever wrong with your data, but the jump looked like damage and has worried people for months. Denoising and sharpening change noise, not brightness - so the preview now simply holds its aim through them: same view, suddenly cleaner.   • Final Polish greets you properly. Its buttons stack by design - so the scary “doubles the effect” warning is gone, replaced by a friendly note that blesses the layering.
  • All three caught by the wizard himself in a single night of field processing - and shipped before the sun came up, because holding back a fix this kind felt wrong.

The mono combiner asks its own version of the question and even relabels its slots (“S → red, H → green, O → blue”) - the eternal which-file-goes-where question, answered where it’s asked.
  • The Wizard’s Way: see both, pick one. The gold-bordered button on the Big Stretch (and step 1) fills the screen with two live previews - linked and unlinked, side by side, neutrally labelled - and you simply click the one that looks right. On the stretch page the panes show your actual stretch both ways, so what you pick is exactly what you get; changing your mind afterwards re-applies instantly.
  • Wizard’s Curves - Colour Transmutation Spells. Five of the wizard’s own finishing curves as one-click stackable spells: Yellow to Copper & Gold, Blue to Teal, Dynamic Pop, Add Warmth, Cool Down. And every spell carries a 📖 button that opens the recipe inside the Curves window, fully editable - see the move, learn the move, make it yours.
  • Deep learned a second stance: “keep colours”. Classic Deep balances each channel itself (perfect for palettes and neutral skies); the new sibling digs just as deep while preserving your field’s true colour balance - golden Milky Way fields stay golden.
  • Normal is stronger now (150, the community-classic value). If you loved the gentler look, Custom 50 recreates it exactly - the Custom dial even marks the spot, and it now travels logarithmically so equal drag means equal visual change.
  • Auto Colour Balance learned some humility. On fields whose brightest stars are genuinely warm, it used to “correct” honest star colour into a full green cast (found the hard way, twice, on the same stubborn dust field). Corrections are now bounded to ±12% - a field’s true colour is not a calibration error - and a lurking maths bug that could blank a channel died in the same surgery.
  • Flow, everywhere: the step numbers up top are clickable; going back asks whether to rewind or keep your image as-is; finished steps offer a gold “Done here - Next Step”; already-applied steps warn before you double-run an AI tool; and the live console is on by default (it’s the app’s black box recorder - off-switch in Settings).

The one that digs deeper - and learns spells

  • The Deep Stretch. Last release we promised “round one” on very faint data - this is round two, and it’s a new engine. Instead of stretching harder (which, we measured, just brightens the sky), Deep anchors to your data: it solves its own white point, black point and strength from the image itself. Faint dust fields open fully, M51-class galaxies reveal their tidal halos, and bright cores stay unblown. Thanks to Lee Polikoff, whose unstretchable stack started this whole hunt.   • Wizard’s Curves - Colour Transmutation Spells. The all-night Mac stakeout

  • The real Mac bug, caught red-handed. Last release we woke the app from its nap - a true fix, but not the whole story. This time, armed with his own MacBook and Apple's diagnostic tools at 3am, Luke caught the deeper culprit in the act: on modern macOS, the app's “refresh the screen” call could get trapped re-drawing the window forever, so it never noticed GraXpert had finished - minutes ago. The interface looked alive; the part that watches for results wasn't. Fixed for good. Field numbers from the stakeout: background extraction 3 seconds, denoise 34 seconds - steps that used to “hang” for 20+ minutes. Mike Frazier's zoom-trick observation was the clue that cracked both chapters of this saga - poking the window was literally breaking the spell.
  • Denoising switched to GraXpert's 2.0.0 AI model on every platform - dramatically faster (about 40× on Apple Silicon), with output quality we couldn't tell apart from the newer model in side-by-side tests. Same model everywhere, so tutorials match your results whatever you run.
  • The Big Stretch reaches deeper into very faint stacks - data scaled into the bottom sliver of the numeric range (thanks to Lee Polikoff's file) used to come out as flat grey mud at any strength; the white-point logic now recognises real signal and opens it up. Honest note: this is round one. On the deepest data there's still more in the tank than a 1000-strength stretch pulls out - deeper reach is actively in the works.
  • One engine at a time, and the app says so - starting a second AI tool or changing steps mid-run now gets a friendly “hang tight” instead of a silent gamble with your working image. The console also narrates honestly: when the result file starts landing on disk, how big it's grown, and exactly when it's collected.
  • Quieter and tougher - the app no longer burns a full CPU core while waiting on an engine, never mistakes an old output file for a fresh one, and if its own plumbing ever fails, the full error prints right in the console ready to copy-paste to us.

17 JULY 2026 · REV B

The one where the app was asleep

  • The great Mac mystery, solved - the app was literally napping. macOS puts idle-looking apps into “App Nap”, and waiting for GraXpert is exactly when AstroWizard looks idle. GraXpert finished its work fine; AstroWizard dozed until you clicked something - which is why zooming or pressing Next Step made runs “suddenly complete”. The app now opts out of App Nap entirely. Full credit to Mike Frazier, whose zoom-trick observation cracked the case, and Rick, who called it exactly: “AstroWizard is not seeing the processing has completed.”
  • Stackers with messy headers can't break saves anymore - malformed header cards (like ASTAP's broken CALSTAT) are now repaired on the fly instead of aborting your crop. Thanks shreddybear.
  • macOS quarantine check: if GraXpert.app still carries the downloaded-app flag, the console now says so before running, with the exact one-line cure. Thanks Greg.
  • Windows shortcut trap: pointing Settings at a desktop shortcut (.lnk) instead of the real program now gets an orange warning and plain instructions, instead of a cryptic “not a valid win32 application”.
